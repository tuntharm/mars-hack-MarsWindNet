03

First experiment: XGBoost.

What can a preliminary baseline learn from the station readings?

Preliminary hackathon baseline

An initial baseline built under hackathon time constraints. The current run did not learn the held-out behaviour adequately; wind and dust predictions remain largely flat. Further diagnosis and tuning are needed.

XGBoost uses the simultaneous station readings to estimate city-centre u, v and dust. Its chronological split uses 70% for training, 15% for validation and 15% for testing.

What we learned This baseline does not reproduce the held-out behaviour adequately. The plots show poor prediction quality; they do not establish numerical non-convergence or its cause.

04

Second experiment: learning from recent history.

Does giving the model a sequence help it interpret changing conditions?

Preliminary LSTM experiment

The LSTM receives the previous 60 time steps of four-station readings and estimates the city-centre values at the current step. With one-second samples, that is 60 seconds of input history—not a 60-second forecast.

The script uses a chronological 80% training / 20% test split. The existing plot labels “Measured” refer to the synthetic simulator reference, not measurements from physical sensors.

What we learned Adding temporal history is a different modelling approach, but these results remain preliminary. We cannot claim superiority over XGBoost: the experiments use different test splits.

05

From point estimates to a map.

What does a coloured map actually represent?

The maps below combine four simulated station values with one LSTM-predicted city-centre value. The plotting script fills the space between them using linear interpolation, with nearest-neighbour values outside their convex hull.

Interpolated display · not a full-field ML prediction

What this tells us A smooth contour is not evidence of spatial prediction accuracy. A learned full-field result needs grid-level training targets and independent validation.

06

What remains to prove.

How do we turn an exploratory experiment into a reliable model?

01 / EVALUATE FAIRLY

Match the experiments.

Compare models on the same time windows, inputs and targets. Diagnose errors and test against simple baselines before claiming an improvement.

02 / GROUND IN PHYSICS

Bring in genuine CFD.

Generate and validate flow around the agreed settlement geometry, with documented boundary conditions and solver assumptions.

03 / CONNECT THE FIELD

Predict across the city.

Train for spatial outputs, check unseen wind conditions and connect compatible numerical fields to the interactive city.

The aim is a shared picture of the environment. These experiments show the first steps—and the evidence still needed.
